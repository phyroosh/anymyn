# ANYMYN — Retention Engineering Manual (V1)
## The Science of Maximizing Viewer Retention, Watch Time, and Audience Satisfaction

> **The Core Rule:**  
> Never confuse attention with chaos.  
> Do not use endless cuts, exaggerated sound effects, misleading clickbait, or artificial suspense.  
> **The viewer must continuously receive a reason to stay.**  
> Our goal is not simply to make people watch longer. It is to make every second worth watching.

---

## 1. The Core Philosophy of Retention Engineering

Modern viewers scroll quickly not because they have short attention spans, but because they have low tolerance for filler, confusion, and disrespect of their time. When content is clear, surprising, visually captivating, and continuously rewarding, viewers stay.

In ANYMYN, retention is an engineering discipline embedded into every stage of production:
1. **Curiosity before explanation:** Never answer a question before making the viewer genuinely care about the answer.
2. **Micro-payoffs throughout:** Never hoard the payoff for the last 5 seconds while subjecting the viewer to 55 seconds of dry setup. Deliver satisfying insights every 8–12 seconds.
3. **Physical visual analogies:** Keep the eye engaged with tangible transformations on our signature ivory stage rather than abstract graphs or static talking cards.
4. **The Curious 10-Year-Old Test:** If a 10-year-old cannot understand the core idea within 10 seconds, the explanation is overcomplicated. Rewrite it.

## 2. Phase 1 — Understanding the Viewer (Audience Modeling)

Before writing a single line of script, answer these six questions in the project research:

1. **Who is the intended viewer?**  
   Curious general viewers scrolling through mobile feeds. Assume zero prior background in economics, finance, or trading.
2. **What do they already understand about the topic?**  
   Identify common everyday experiences (e.g. going grocery shopping, putting money in a bank, watching prices rise).
3. **What misconception or unanswered question might they have?**  
   Pinpoint the counter-intuitive flaw in common thinking (e.g., *"If I have $100 in my wallet, I still have $100, right?"* or *"Compounding growth moves in a straight line"*).
4. **Why would this information matter to them right now?**  
   Establish tangible personal stakes (their savings, their time, their future purchasing power).
5. **What specific thing will they understand after watching?**  
   Define one crystal-clear takeaway that can be expressed in one sentence.
6. **What would make them immediately swipe away?**  
   List and actively eliminate risks: academic definitions, generic talking heads, sluggish openings, or visual clutter.

## 3. Phase 2 — Hook Engineering (The First 3 Seconds)

The first three seconds determine whether 70% of viewers stay or swipe.

### What is STRICTLY FORBIDDEN in the Opening:
- ❌ *"Welcome back to the channel."*
- ❌ *"Today we're going to talk about..."*
- ❌ Channel intros, logo stings, or bumper animations.
- ❌ Academic textbook definitions (*"Inflation is defined as a sustained increase in the general price level..."*).
- ❌ Slow atmospheric b-roll without a clear, immediate proposition.

### The Three Hook Archetypes
For every video, develop three distinct opening candidates:

| Hook Archetype | Psychological Mechanism | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **A. The Curiosity Hook** | Poses a striking, unanswered question that exposes a gap in everyday knowledge. | *"Why does a hundred-dollar bill buy less food today than it did yesterday, even though the number never changed?"* |
| **B. The Contradiction Hook** | Presents two true facts that seem to contradict each other, demanding resolution. | *"Your savings didn't lose a single dollar this year. But somehow, you're forty percent poorer."* |
| **C. The Concrete-Stakes Hook** | Grounds the idea in an immediate, tangible dollar amount, object, or consequence. | *"Imagine leaving ten thousand dollars in a drawer for twenty years. When you open it, nearly half the money has vanished into thin air."* |

### Hook Evaluation Matrix (7 Criteria)
Evaluate the 3 candidates on a 1–5 scale across:
1. **Immediate Comprehension:** Can a viewer grasp the sentence while scrolling casually?
2. **Curiosity Pull:** Does it trigger an involuntary *"Wait, how?"*
3. **Target Audience Relevance:** Does it affect an ordinary person's life?
4. **Visual Potential:** Does it pair with a striking 3D prop or physical action?
5. **Specificity:** Uses concrete numbers/objects rather than vague generalities.
6. **Credibility:** 100% true; zero deceptive sensationalism.
7. **Eventual Payoff:** The video can completely deliver on the opening promise.

Select the winning hook and keep the alternatives recorded in `storyboard.md`.

## 4. Phase 3 — Curiosity Architecture & Open Loops

Storytelling is tension and release. ANYMYN structures scripts around the universal rhythm:

$$\text{QUESTION} \longrightarrow \text{DEMONSTRATION} \longrightarrow \text{EXPLANATION} \longrightarrow \text{CONSEQUENCE} \longrightarrow \text{NEW QUESTION} \longrightarrow \text{PAYOFF}$$

### Open Loop Management:
- **What is an Open Loop?** An intriguing question or tension introduced before its answer is revealed.
- **Rule of 1–2:** Maintain a maximum of **1 or 2 open loops** simultaneously. Too many loops create confusion; zero loops eliminate suspense.
- **Mandatory Closure:** Every single open loop introduced **must receive a complete, satisfying answer** before the video ends.
- **No Stall Tactics:** Never postpone answers merely to drag out watch time. Give early micro-answers, then deepen the stakes.

## 5. Phase 4 — Narrative Progression & Micro-Payoffs

Divide the video into distinct **narrative beats** (typically 6–10 beats per 60-second video).

Every beat must deliver at least one of the **Seven Value Deliverables**:
1. A new, useful piece of information.
2. A physical visual demonstration.
3. A surprising, counter-intuitive comparison.
4. A concrete, relatable example.
5. A widespread misconception corrected.
6. An important financial consequence revealed.
7. A satisfying answer to an earlier question.

### Pacing Rules:
- **Lean Duration:** Choose the shortest duration that communicates the idea properly. Never stretch a 45-second explanation into a 70-second video with repetitive phrasing.
- **Purposeful Visual Beats:** A visual beat is not a random flashy cut every 1.5 seconds. A visual beat is a meaningful change:
  - An object transformation (e.g. basket items ghosting out).
  - A camera drift or scale shift.
  - A dramatic numerical reveal ($100 ➔ $180.61).
  - A physical balance tilt.
  - An intentional moment of pause and stillness.

---

## 6. Phase 5 — Retention-First Scriptwriting

Write for the human ear and voice, not for a printed essay.

### Scriptwriting Standards:
- **Curious 10-Year-Old Vocabulary:** Use short, punchy sentences and everyday words. Avoid academic terminology (*"nominal purchasing power decay"* ➔ *"what that money can actually buy"*).
- **Spoken Numerical Formatting:** All numbers must be written as spoken words for natural ElevenLabs narration:
  - *Say:* `one hundred eighty-one dollars`, not `$181`.
  - *Say:* `three percent every year`, not `3% per annum`.
- **The Pruning Rule:** Read every sentence aloud. If a sentence does not attract attention, build understanding, or deliver a payoff, **delete it**.

## 7. Phase 6 — Visual Retention Engineering

The visual design is not background decoration; it is the physical proof of the argument.

### Permanent Visual Invariants:
- **Ivory Grid Studio:** Permanent warm ivory wall (`#F3EFE5`), soft radial key spotlight (`#FAF7F0`), and warm cream floor (`#E9DFC9`) with perspective grid lines compressing toward the horizon.
- **Cardless Physical Metaphors:** Tangible dimensional objects (banknotes, wicker baskets, brass scales, sculptural medallions) rather than generic UI cards or floating data boxes.
- **Monumental Typography:** High-contrast serif headlines (78px–92px), crisp hierarchy, zero mobile clutter.

### For Every Visual Beat, Answer:
1. *What should the viewer look at?* (Single unambiguous focal point).
2. *What should change visually?* (Motion, transform, or reveal).
3. *What should the viewer understand?* (The concept linked to the visual).
4. *What curiosity does this visual create?* (The pull into the next beat).
5. *What is the visual payoff?* (The satisfying resolution).
6. *Why should they continue watching?* (The unanswered tension).

### Progressive Disclosure:
Never dump an entire chart or formula on screen at once. Reveal in stages:
1. Show the starting baseline.
2. Show the change taking place.
3. Reveal the unexpected consequence.

## 8. Phase 7 — The Retention Map (`retention_map.md`)

For every new production, author a dedicated `retention_map.md` in `videos/<video-slug>/retention_map.md` using `retention_map_template.md`.

It tracks:
- Beat # & Timestamp.
- Exact Spoken Narration.
- Viewer Question.
- Information Delivered.
- Visual Action & Prop Dynamics.
- Attention Mechanism.
- Micro-Payoff.
- Next Curiosity.
- Sound Design & Audio FX.
- Open Loop Status (Opened vs Closed).

## 10. Phase 9 — The Retention Quality Gate (Score $\ge 4$)

Before presenting the voiceover script to the user, rate the package across 8 categories (1 to 5):

| Category | Passing Standard ($\ge 4$) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **1. Opening Strength** | Hook creates instant stakes within 3 seconds; zero fluff or introductions. |
| **2. Clarity (10-Yr-Old Test)** | A beginner understands the premise immediately; no unexplained jargon. |
| **3. Curiosity Pull** | Every section naturally pulls the viewer into the next beat. |
| **4. Beat Progression** | Every single beat delivers new information or a visual transformation. Zero padding. |
| **5. Visual Novelty** | Scene transitions, prop changes, or scale shifts refresh attention organically. |
| **6. Information Density** | Every sentence earns its place; concise, punchy spoken delivery. |
| **7. Payoff Satisfaction** | The conclusion completely answers the opening curiosity with high-impact clarity. |
| **8. Credibility & Accuracy** | 100% verified math (Python Decimal) and qualified claims. Zero deceptive clickbait. |

**Rule:** If any category scores below 4, revise the script and storyboard before proceeding to the voiceover handoff.

## 11. Phase 10 — Platform-Specific Optimization

### YouTube Shorts:
- **New August 24, 2026 Measurement:** YouTube counts a view when playback begins. Compare *Engaged Views* and *Stayed-to-Watch Rate* alongside Average View Duration (AVD) and Average Percentage Viewed (APV) to evaluate genuine hook performance.
- Focus on minimizing the first 3-second swipe rate and creating strong rewatch moments.

### Instagram Reels:
- Measure *Shares / Sends per Reach*. Financial content succeeds on Reels when viewers share it as an actionable insight or eye-opening realization with friends.
- Optimize for high save rates and clean mobile typography within safe margins.

### TikTok:
- Optimize for completion rate and rapid narrative velocity. The first 1.5 seconds must present an immediate visual or conceptual pattern interrupt.

## 12. Phase 11 — Post-Publication Learning & Controlled Experimentation

To ensure continuous improvement:
1. Use `analytics/retention_review_template.md` to analyze completed videos once published analytics are provided by the user.
2. **Single-Variable Experimentation:** When testing improvements across consecutive videos, vary only **one major variable** at a time:
   - Hook archetype (Contradiction vs Concrete-Stakes).
   - Video duration (45s vs 60s vs 75s).
   - Visual pace (frequent object morphing vs held focus).
   - Ending payoff style.
3. Record findings in `analytics/` and feed validated lessons directly into future scriptwriting.
